# Reading list — BUSINESS · Value-driven business model

> The commercial side: creating, delivering and capturing value, the measurement of willingness to pay, and the pricing, licensing and contracting mechanisms that decide who keeps the margin.
>
> One section per note, numbered `GG-x`. Where a note needed no source beyond its own
> **References**, there is no section here — the note is self-sufficient.

## How to use this list

- **Marks.** Type and priority marks (`★★★`, `[Book]`, `[EN]`) are defined once, in the MOC under *Reading marks*. The rule that matters: `★★★` means read or skim before you quote the note in an interview.
- **Titles** are kept in their published form and never translated; descriptions are English.
- **Nothing is asserted.** Where a citation could not be confirmed against a publisher, DOI, arXiv record or the issuing body, it is marked `VERIFY`.

## 10-1 · Value-driven business model — methodology, process, tools and best practice
### Value, strategy and capture

- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Brandenburger, A. M. and Stuart, H. W. "Value-based business strategy." *Journal of Economics and Management Strategy*, 1996.
  - The formal basis of the whole note: value created as willingness to pay minus opportunity cost, added value as the difference you make, and why creation and capture are separate questions. Check the volume and pages before citing them.
- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Teece, D. J. "Profiting from technological innovation: implications for integration, collaboration, licensing and public policy." *Research Policy* 15(6), 1986.
  - Appropriability regimes and complementary assets — the paper that explains why inventors so often do not get paid. Read it before you design a licensing strategy.
- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Teece, D. J. "Business Models, Business Strategy and Innovation." *Long Range Planning* 43(2-3), 2010.
  - The definition used throughout: the business model as the architecture of value creation, delivery and capture.
- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Chesbrough, H. and Rosenbloom, R. S. "The role of the business model in capturing value from innovation: evidence from Xerox Corporation's technology spin-off companies." *Industrial and Corporate Change* 11(3), 2002.
  - The empirical version of "created but not captured", with the spin-offs as the evidence.
- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Zott, C. and Amit, R. "Business Model Design: An Activity System Perspective." *Long Range Planning* 43(2-3), 2010.
  - The activity-system view and the four themes — novelty, lock-in, complementarities, efficiency — with the evidence that novel designs improve performance.
- **[Paper · ★★ · EN]** Amit, R. and Zott, C. "Value creation in e-business." *Strategic Management Journal* 22(6-7), 2001; and "Creating value through business model innovation." *MIT Sloan Management Review*, 2012.
  - The theoretical roots of the four themes, and the later argument that business model and product-market strategy are complements, not substitutes.

### Business model design tools

- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Osterwalder, A. and Pigneur, Y. *Business Model Generation: A Handbook for Visionaries, Game Changers, and Challengers*. Wiley, 2010 · ISBN 978-0-470-87641-1
  - The nine-block canvas, and the discipline that every cell is a hypothesis to be tested rather than a fact to be filed.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Osterwalder, A., Pigneur, Y., Bernarda, G. and Smith, A. *Value Proposition Design: How to Create Products and Services Customers Want*. Wiley, 2014.
  - The Value Proposition Canvas: jobs, pains and gains against products, pain relievers and gain creators, with fit checked item by item. One canvas per segment.
- **[Book · ★★ · EN]** Maurya, A. *Running Lean: Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works*. O'Reilly, 3rd ed. 2022.
  - The Lean Canvas for start-up contexts: problem and unfair advantage first, metrics explicit.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Gassmann, O., Frankenberger, K. and Csik, M. *The Business Model Navigator: 55 Models That Will Revolutionise Your Business*. FT Publishing / Pearson, 2014.
  - The magic triangle, the 55-pattern library, and the finding that about 90% of business model innovation is recombination. A vocabulary and a workshop tool, not a decision procedure.
- **[Article · ★★★ · EN]** Johnson, M. W., Christensen, C. M. and Kagermann, H. "Reinventing Your Business Model." *Harvard Business Review*, December 2008.
  - Four interlocking elements and the two tests that decide whether a model can be repeated. Short; re-read it once a year.
- **[Article · ★★★ · EN]** Kim, W. C. and Mauborgne, R. "Value Innovation: The Strategic Logic of High Growth." *Harvard Business Review*, 1997; and *Blue Ocean Strategy*, Harvard Business School Press, 2005.
  - Value innovation as the simultaneous pursuit of differentiation and low cost, the four actions framework, and the ERRC grid. The authors' insistence on research with buyers and non-customers is what separates use from misuse.

### Customer value and pricing

- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Nagle, T. T., Hogan, J. E. and Zale, J. *The Strategy and Tactics of Pricing: A Guide to Growing More Profitably*. Routledge, 5th ed., 2016.
  - The standard codification of value-based pricing: economic value to the customer, price structure, value communication, policy, level and implementation. The Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (1976) and conjoint analysis appear here as tools.
- **[Article · ★★★ · EN]** Marn, M. V. and Rosiello, R. L. "Managing Price, Gaining Profit." *Harvard Business Review*, September-October 1992.
  - The 2,463-company study behind the 11.1% figure, and the original argument that price beats volume and cost as a profit lever.
- **[Report · ★★★ · EN]** Marn, M. V., Roegner, E. V. and Zawada, C. C. "The power of pricing." *McKinsey Quarterly*, 2003.
  - The S&P 1500 income statement, the ~8% result, the 18.7% volume arithmetic, and the pocket-price waterfall with its long list of leakages.
- **[Article · ★★ · EN]** Baker, W. L., Marn, M. V. and Zawada, C. C. "Building a better pricing infrastructure." *McKinsey Quarterly*, August 2010.
  - The 8.7% Global 1200 figure, and the sequence that makes pricing durable: processes, organisation, performance management, then systems.
- **[Article · ★★ · EN]** McKinsey and Company. "Using big data to make better pricing decisions."
  - The modern restatement of the 8.7% figure, the estimate that up to 30% of pricing decisions miss the best price, and the four steps for granular B2B pricing.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Ulwick, A. W. *What Customers Want: Using Outcome-Driven Innovation to Create Breakthrough Products and Services*. McGraw-Hill, 2005; and "Turn Customer Input into Innovation." *Harvard Business Review*, January 2002.
  - Job maps, outcome statements and the opportunity algorithm. Take the method seriously and the success-rate claim sceptically.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Christensen, C. M., Hall, T., Dillon, K. and Duncan, D. S. *Competing Against Luck: The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice*. HarperBusiness, 2016.
  - The job as the unit of analysis, circumstances over demographics, and the milkshake study as the worked example.
- **[Book · ★★ · EN]** Moesta, B. *Demand-Side Sales 101: Stop Selling and Help Your Customers Make Progress*. Idea Bite Press, 2020.
  - Switch interviews, the purchase timeline and the four forces of progress. The interviewing companion to JTBD.

### Financial architecture

- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Rappaport, A. *Creating Shareholder Value: The New Standard for Business Performance*. Free Press, 1986; 2nd ed. 1998.
  - The seven value drivers and the rule that growth only creates value above the cost of capital.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Copeland, T., Koller, T. and Murrin, J. *Valuation: Measuring and Managing the Value of Companies*. McKinsey and Company / Wiley.
  - The value driver tree in practical form: ROIC, growth and free cash flow, and what to do with the spread.
- **[Book · ★★ · EN]** Stern, J. M. and Shiely, J. S. *The EVA Challenge: Implementing Value-Added Change in an Organization*. Wiley, 2001.
  - Economic profit as a management system, and the capital-charge discipline that keeps a model honest about the assets it consumes.
- **[Article · ★★★ · EN]** McGrath, R. G. and MacMillan, I. C. "Discovery-Driven Planning." *Harvard Business Review*, July-August 1995.
  - Writing the P&L backwards from required profit to allowable cost, and funding assumptions in order of risk. The technique behind the process section.

### Recurring revenue, platforms and services

- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Vandermerwe, S. and Rada, J. "Servitization of business: adding value by adding services." *European Management Journal* 6(4), 1988.
  - Where the term comes from, and the move from goods to goods-plus-services.
- **[Paper · ★★★ · EN]** Gebauer, H., Fleisch, E. and Friedli, T. "Overcoming the service paradox in manufacturing companies." *European Management Journal* 23(1), 2005.
  - The service paradox named and diagnosed: heavy service investment with profit that does not follow.
- **[Book · ★★ · EN]** Baines, T. and Lightfoot, H. *Made to Serve: How Manufacturers Can Compete Through Servitization and Product Service Systems*. Wiley, 2013.
  - Base, intermediate and advanced services, and the organisational change each requires.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Parker, G. G., Van Alstyne, M. W. and Choudary, S. P. *Platform Revolution*. W. W. Norton, 2016.
  - Multi-sided platform design: which side to subsidise, the take rate, and the failure modes of disintermediation.
- **[Report · ★★ · EN]** Bessemer Venture Partners. *State of the Cloud*; David Skok, *SaaS metrics* (forentrepreneurs.com); SaaS Capital annual benchmarking survey.
  - The benchmark conventions: LTV:CAC, CAC payback, net revenue retention, the Rule of 40 — with the warning that they are clusters, not targets.
- **[Article · ★★ · EN]** Feld, B. "The Rule of 40." 2015.
  - Where the rule comes from, and why growth and margin must be read together.

### Practice in mature organisations

- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Cooper, R. and Slagmulder, R. *Target Costing and Value Engineering*. Productivity Press, 1997.
  - Price first, profit second, allowable cost as a design constraint. The most concrete value-driven practice in the literature, and the one engineers find easiest to adopt.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Morgan, J. M. and Liker, J. K. *The Toyota Product Development System: Integrating People, Process and Technology*. Productivity Press, 2006 · ISBN 978-1-56327-282-3
  - The chief engineer system, set-based concurrent engineering, obeya and front-loading, with the Prius programme as the worked example.
- **[Book · ★★ · EN]** Womack, J. P. and Jones, D. T. *Lean Thinking: Banish Waste and Create Wealth in Your Corporation*. Simon and Schuster, 1996 · ISBN 978-0-684-81003-5
  - Value defined by the customer, and the value stream mapped from the customer backwards.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Bryar, C. and Carr, B. *Working Backwards: Insights, Stories, and Secrets from Inside Amazon*. St. Martin's Press, 2021 · ISBN 978-1-250-26702-8
  - Working backwards, single-threaded ownership, input metrics, and mechanisms instead of intentions.
- **[Report · ★★★ · EN]** Adobe Systems. *Adobe's Cloud Innovations Drive Strong Q4 and FY2013 Financial Results*, 2013-12-12.
  - The primary numbers for a designed subscription transition: revenue $4.06B against $4.40B, 1.439M paid subscriptions, Creative ARR $768M, and the pre-announced growth target.
- **[Report · ★★★ · EN]** Rolls-Royce. *Rolls-Royce celebrates 50th anniversary of Power-by-the-Hour*, press release, 2012-10-30; and *TotalCare*, corporate story, 2017.
  - The primary account of the 1962 origin, CorporateCare from 2002, per-flying-hour charging, services as roughly half of revenue, and engine health monitoring as the data loop.
- **[Report · ★★★ · EN]** National Development and Reform Commission of the People's Republic of China. *Decision on Qualcomm's abuse of market dominance*, February 2015.
  - The primary text for the 6.088 billion RMB fine and the remedies, including the 65%-of-net-selling-price royalty base. The clearest statement of where royalty-based capture meets competition law.
- **[Filing · ★★★ · EN]** Arm Holdings plc. *Form F-1 registration statement*, 2023.
  - The primary source for the licence-plus-royalty split, shipment volumes and revenue by type. Use it rather than secondary summaries when a royalty percentage matters.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Porter, M. E. and Teisberg, E. O. *Redefining Health Care: Creating Value-Based Competition on Results*. Harvard Business School Press, 2006; Porter, M. E., "Value-Based Health Care Delivery." *Annals of Surgery* 248(4), 2008.
  - The largest worked attempt to run an industry on a value metric, with the six-part agenda.
- **[Article · ★★★ · EN]** Reinhardt, U. E. "Health Reform: Porter and Teisberg's Utopian Vision." *Health Affairs* Forefront, 2006.
  - The serious critique: outcomes are multidimensional and gameable, risk adjustment is imperfect, and payment reform meets incumbent politics. Read it beside the book.
- **[Book · ★★★ · EN]** Ries, E. *The Lean Startup*. Crown Business, 2011 · ISBN 978-0-307-88789-4
  - Build-measure-learn, and why an experiment is only valid with a pre-declared hypothesis and a stopping rule.
