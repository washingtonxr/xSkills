## WLAN — new note 02-14, Wi-Fi fundamentals from the two Wi-Fi 7 books

### What was written
- `knowledge-graph/WLAN/02-14 WLAN - Wi-Fi Fundamentals - History, Architecture, PHY,
  MAC and the Road to Wi-Fi 7.md` — 1535 lines, 23 Mermaid diagrams, 34 tables,
  30 references. Built from *Wi-Fi 7 In Depth* (Henry, Gupta, Hart, Smith; Cisco Press
  2024, 385 pp.) and *Wi-Fi 7: Principles, Technology, and Applications* (Cheng et al.;
  Springer 2025, XXVI+464 pp., DOI 10.1007/978-981-97-9026-5).
- Sections 0-19: what Wi-Fi is and why its MAC differs from 802.3's, the ALOHA-to-802.11be
  history, the BSS/ESS architecture and frame taxonomy, the seven-phase join, CSMA/CA and
  EDCA, the numerology, the rate equation, MIMO and sounding, aggregation and Block Ack,
  Wi-Fi 6 as the hinge, Wi-Fi 7 PHY (320 MHz / 4096-QAM / MRU / puncturing / EHT PPDU),
  MLO and the five client modes, the latency tools, deployment, product test (EVT/DVT/PVT),
  certification, 5G convergence, trends, and an interview package.
- The note's spine is the **rate arithmetic**: `R = Nsd x Nss x log2(M) x Rc / Tsym`,
  worked for 600 Mbps / 6.93 Gbps / 9.61 Gbps / 23.06 Gbps. That table explains why Wi-Fi 6
  raised the peak only 1.39x (the symbol grew 3.78x) and why the 46 Gbps figure is the
  16-stream arithmetic the standard does not contain.

## WLAN — new note 02-15, the transceiver from superheterodyne to the AI-native radio

### What was written
- `knowledge-graph/WLAN/02-15 WLAN - The Transceiver - From Superheterodyne to Wi-Fi 7 and the AI-Native
  Radio.md` — 1056 lines, 14 Mermaid diagrams, 25 tables, ~40 references, English only.
- Thesis: the standard does not specify a radio, it specifies what the waveform must look like at the antenna, so
  every EVM and sensitivity number is a purchase order to the analog designer.
- Sections 0-20: six eras; the chip partition (SoC / FEM / antenna) and why the PA cannot live in the digital node;
  five jobs and the metric each owns; noise floor, sensitivity and EVM budgets; superheterodyne (Armstrong 1918,
  WaveLAN 1990) with the image arithmetic; zero-IF and its four impairments, including the standard's own exclusion
  of the centre subcarrier and +-3 neighbours from the EVM test; DPD and the three calibration loops; the
  generation-by-generation transceiver table; TX and RX chains with numbers; the preamble-cost table; MIMO and MLO
  costs; power and heat; coexistence; 802.11bf sensing and the range-resolution arithmetic; AI today (what is
  standardised vs what is hidden in firmware); the 16 us SIFS wall against a 1 ms neural receiver; AI traffic and the
  802.11bn ELR PPDU as the receiver-first turn; five silicon bets; ten numbers / ten answers / ten corrections.

### Numbers that carry the note (all sourced)
- EVM: -5 dB BPSK to -38 dB 4096-QAM (802.11be 36.3.19.4); 3 dB per 4x constellation, where theory asks 6 dB.
- Sensitivity scales 3 dB per doubling of bandwidth (noise floor -101 dBm at 20 MHz, -88.9 at 320 MHz);
  IEEE MCS13 floor -46 dBm at 20 MHz, -34 at 320; vendor typical values 8-12 dB better, implying NF ~3 dB.
- Range table: 320 MHz MCS13 reaches ~2.5 m at 30 dBm EIRP; 20 MHz MCS0 reaches ~250 m.
- Preamble ~56 us vs 4.2 us of payload at 320 MHz MCS13 = 93% overhead (the fact that explains 802.11bn ELR).
- Neural receiver: 730k weights, <1 ms on A100 (arXiv 2312.02601) vs 13.6 us symbol and 16 us SIFS.
