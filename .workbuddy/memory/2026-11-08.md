### Facts verified while writing (and where they came from)

- **60 m landed in the US only on 2025-12-09** — FCC Report and Order, 5351.5–5366.5 kHz secondary,
  9.15 W ERP, General class and above, 2.8 kHz max bandwidth, four legacy channels at 5332/5348/5373/
  5405 kHz retained at 100 W ERP. WRC-15 decided it in 2015; the national rule took a decade.
- **FT8 is LDPC(174,91), not (174,87)** — the 2017 WSJT-X 1.8.0 parameters (75-bit payload,
  LDPC(174,87)) were replaced on 2018-12-10 by WSJT-X 2.0 with a 77-bit payload and LDPC(174,91).
  Threshold ≈ −21 dB in 2.5 kHz, not −20 dB. This is now a correction row.
- **US licence counts inverted in 2022**: peak 770,217 (2022) → 745,571 (end-2024) → 734,841
  (April 2026); Technician is 49.1% and took almost all of the 2025 loss, Extra *gained*. ARRL
  membership ≈16% of licensees (from ~24% in 2000); median member age 68 in 2019.
- **China MIIT Order No. 67** (published 2024-01-18, in force 2024-03-01): A 30–3000 MHz ≤25 W;
  B <30 MHz <15 W or >30 MHz ≤25 W after 6 months at A; C <30 MHz ≤1000 W after 18 months holding an
  HF-authorised licence; licence ≤5 years; log retention 2 years; callsign at least every 10 minutes.
- **FCC 23-93** (adopted 2023-11-13, effective 2024) removed the HF symbol-rate limit and set a
  2.8 kHz bandwidth limit — the reason PACTOR-IV and wide data modes became legal without a waiver.
- **OSCAR 1**: 1961-12-12, 10 kg, 140 mW, 144.983 MHz, "HI" for 22 days, $68 out-of-pocket, first
  non-government satellite and first piggyback payload.

## 02-18 — AI in Wi-Fi and the 802.11bx WIN vision

New WLAN note: `02-18 WLAN - AI in Wi-Fi - Learning Inside the Protocol and the 802.11bx Vision for PHY and
MAC.md` (1,432 lines, 12 Mermaid, 33 tables, 23 references). Built from the two papers the user named —
Wilhelmi/Kosek-Szott/Szott/Bellalta, *IEEE 802.11bx — WLAN Intelligent Networking (WIN)*, arXiv:2610.07900
(Oct 2026), and Karamyshev/Levitsky/Khorov, *Towards Wi-Fi 9*, Probl. Inf. Transm. 61(4):383–406, 2025 — plus
the IEEE 802.11 WIN SG and AIO SG pages for status.

Structure: three roles (AI as protocol / platform / traffic) → paper trail with dates → driver numbers → PHY
section (four time loops; CSI-compression arithmetic; neural receiver behind the compliant interface;
prediction; interference classification; pilotless and learned constellations blocked by the PPDU/conformance
model) → MAC section (four decisions and which are safe to learn; three levels of learned channel access; why a
learned MAC cannot be bounded; AC_AI full case study with derived AIFS/backoff arithmetic; determinism
enforced outside the learner; multi-AP beyond two APs; power and mobile AP) → 802.11bu as a two-resource
scheduler → queueing view of agentic traffic → four gates (gain, compat, **testability without the model**,
privacy) → status table of what is standardised versus a slide → interview package → Anchors → Related →
References.

Independent analysis contributed by the note (not in either paper):

- **AC_AI parameters do not form a total order.** With the paper's values (AIFSN 4, CWmin 8, CWmax 64), mean
  wait before first attempt is 88 µs, i.e. *ahead* of video at 106 µs, because CWmin 8 < 16; at saturation the
  ordering inverts because CWmax 64 > 32. "Between video and best effort" is an average across load.
- **AI uplink is preamble-bound, not rate-bound.** A 4 kbit frame is ~9 µs of payload behind ~40 µs of
  preamble; a classroom's 6 Mb/s is ~1,500 frames/s ≈ 7.5 % airtime of which ~6 % is preamble. Raising MCS
  does nothing; trigger-based OFDMA does.
- **Sounding arithmetic** for the CSI case: 996 reports × ~128 angles × 10 bits ≈ 1.27 Mbit per 8×8/320 MHz
  sounding, i.e. ~148 ms at MCS 0 versus ~0.1 ms at MCS 13 — cost is set by the weakest participant.
- **Testability is the gate that separates receivers from schedulers** — a bit stream can be checked, a choice
  cannot, so 802.11bx will standardise contracts (interfaces, ranges, envelopes) rather than functions.

Three facts corrected against primary sources and recorded in `99 Corrections` (4 rows): the **802.11bx letter
is unsettled** (WIN SG's page commits to none; Karamyshev writes "bX" with a footnote), **no AI feature is
committed** (no PAR before Jan 2027; inclusion "remains uncertain"; 802.11bu is offload, not radio AI), the
**neural receiver cannot sit in the symbol path** (< 1 ms vs 13.6 µs symbol / 16 µs SIFS), and a **fifth AC is
not just a queue** (fixed UP→AC table; 8 TIDs).
